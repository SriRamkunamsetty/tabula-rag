# HEXFALL — Official Rulebook, Edition 1

## 1. Overview

Hexfall is a two-player abstract strategy game played on a 5x5 grid of
square cells, indexed by column letters A-E (left to right) and row numbers
1-5 (bottom to top), so the bottom-left cell is A1 and the top-right cell is
E5. Each player controls seven stones of a single colour: five ordinary
stones called Runners, and two enhanced stones called Towers. The game was
designed for this rulebook and does not correspond to any previously
published game.

## 2. Setup

Player One (Amber) places their seven stones on row 1 in any arrangement of
their choosing. Player Two (Slate) places their seven stones on row 5 in any
arrangement of their choosing. Cell C3, the centre of the board, is marked
as the Well and starts empty. No stone may be placed on the Well during
setup.

Amber always moves first.

## 3. Turn structure

On a player's turn, they must perform exactly one of the following two
actions:

  (a) Move one of their stones one cell orthogonally (north, south, east,
      or west — never diagonally) into an empty adjacent cell.

  (b) Perform a Leap: move one of their stones two cells in a straight
      orthogonal line, jumping directly over one adjacent stone of either
      colour, landing in an empty cell immediately beyond it. The jumped
      stone is not affected or removed by a Leap.

A player who cannot legally move any stone forfeits their turn.

## 4. The Well

The cell C3 (the Well) has a special rule: any stone that enters the Well
becomes Sunk. A Sunk stone is removed from the board immediately and takes
no further part in the game, for either player. A player may voluntarily
move their own stone into the Well; this is legal but sacrifices the stone.

## 5. Capturing

A stone captures an enemy stone by Leaping over it under the ordinary Leap
rule in section 3(b), provided the jumped stone belongs to the opponent.
Landing beyond a friendly stone in a Leap is legal but does not capture it.
A captured stone is removed from the board and returned to its owner's
supply; it does not re-enter the game.

Runners may only capture Runners. Towers may capture either Runners or
Towers. A Runner may never capture a Tower.

## 6. Towers

A Tower moves and Leaps exactly as a Runner does (section 3), but has one
additional ability: once per game, on its own turn, in place of moving, a
Tower may perform a Surge — capturing every enemy Runner in a straight
orthogonal line of sight from the Tower's current cell, up to and including
the third cell in that line, provided no other stone (friendly or enemy)
sits between the Tower and the captured Runners. A Surge cannot capture an
enemy Tower. Each Tower may use its Surge ability only once per game, not
once per turn.

## 7. Victory condition

A player wins immediately when their opponent has no Towers remaining on
the board, either through capture or through a Tower being Sunk in the
Well. Reducing an opponent to zero Runners does not end the game by itself,
provided the opponent still controls at least one Tower.

## 8. Draws

If the same complete board position (same stones, same colours, same
cells) occurs for a third time, with the same player to move each time, the
game is declared a draw. Either player may claim the draw when the
repetition occurs; if neither claims it, play continues.

## 9. Turn limit

A game that reaches move 60 (a "move" being one turn by either player)
without a winner is declared a draw.
